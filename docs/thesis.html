After NEAR Intents froze funds, its volume held. Its public record didn't.

NEAR HACKATHON 2026 · Investment Thesis track · data snapshot to 2026-11-09 · every number below is generated by src/build.py from data/raw/ · charts: the dashboard (docs/index.html).

The bear case. A cross-chain venue whose operator can freeze funds is not trustless, so users will leave the first time it uses that power.

The test. Two freeze stories reached the public three weeks apart. On 2026-09-08 a Zcash user reported $589k held for 50 days. After the Bitget hack, SHIELD blocked more than $50M of transfers tied to the hackers and froze $503k mid-swap (reported 2026-09-29). Then a $3.8M exploit paused the contract (2026-10-01); the funds were returned the next day.

The result. Total volume held: +1.0% against a model built from rival venues and the market, and deposits dipped 12.2%. Its public record did not. On NEAR's chain the share of volume visible as public swaps went from 59% in the summer to 19% by November, and from late October public swap events fell to a fifth of August's level while executed intents doubled.

The catch. Total volume is DefiLlama's figure, and most of it can no longer be checked onchain: run after the fact on public onchain volume, the same model gives -28.6% (p = 0.20, not significant). Why the swaps left public view is not identified: Confidential Intents, contract upgrades on 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-07, and a change in how solvers settle all fit. Intents' summer momentum stopped, and Zcash balances drained a month later.

The bet. The freeze bears were wrong about the reported flow. My reading: being trusted, not trustless, is NEAR's edge. But an investor can no longer check most of Intents' volume on the chain, so the case rests on reported numbers, and revenue must still grow about 5.2× for buybacks to offset new NEAR supply. Position: Long, one tripwire broken.

What the freeze bears got right and wrong

  • Wrong, on reported volume: the flow leaves. DefiLlama's total tracked its model, which is built from rival venues and spot volume, through both events, and deposits dipped 12.2%. The pre-registered freeze-channel test (H4) found no Zcash flight in the month after the freeze.
  • Missed: the public record leaves. The share of volume visible as public swaps fell from about three-fifths to under a fifth, and from late October public swaps fell while executed intents doubled (section 7). Less of the flow can be seen.
  • Partly right: public activity weakens. On public onchain volume the model gives -28.6%, weaker though not significant (p = 0.20). Accounts that had used Intents publicly before September came back less than in any calm period (section 6); they may have left or moved to a less visible route. Intents' summer momentum stopped (section 1). Zcash balances fell past their calm-period limit a month later.
  • Not shown: why. The data does not separate users choosing Confidential Intents, the October upgrades and a change in solver settlement.

The long-term case

NEAR's token earns its value from use, and the use that pays is NEAR Intents: cross-chain swaps whose protocol revenue has bought NEAR on the open market since the February 2026 fee switch. The case for holding NEAR for years is therefore a case about Intents: that its revenue lasts, and that it grows.

  • Growth so far. Volume first peaked at $3.66B in Nov 2025. It then sat between $1.90B and $2.78B a month for nine months, before a $4.80B record in Sep 2026. Oct 2026 was $3.99B: 2.3× the same month a year earlier, when Intents was still ramping up, but only 1.1× the first peak (Exhibit 3 on the dashboard).
  • What growth has to do. NEAR's total supply grew by 31.7M NEAR in the year to 2026-11-09 (2.5%), from NEAR's own supply table on BigQuery (issuance net of burns; circulating supply grew 36.5M including unlocks). If every dollar of protocol revenue bought NEAR at today's price, a year of revenue at the last 30 days' run rate would buy about 6.1M NEAR, 19.4% of new supply. To offset all of it, revenue has to grow about 5.2×.
  • How firm the target is. It moves with two things. NEAR keeps more of each swap now (4.9 bps of volume) than in the calm period (2.6 bps); at the calm share the target is 9.6×. A cheaper NEAR buys more tokens per dollar; at the calm-period price the target is 2.7×. I have not established whether bought NEAR is burned or held.
  • Why durability comes first. A growth case is only worth making if the revenue does not disappear the first time trust is tested. The rest of this document tests exactly that.

The long-term section uses the same snapshot as the rest but was added after the pre-registration (deviation 5).

The stress test

  • Operator discretion (2026-09-29): SHIELD blocked more than $50M of transfers linked to the Bitget hack and froze $503k mid-swap, weeks after a Zcash user reported $589k held for 50 days.
  • Security failure (2026-10-01): a $3.8M exploit paused the contract and halted deposits and withdrawals on eleven networks. The funds were returned on 2026-10-02, per the 16 October postmortem.

Both hit the thing that makes Intents revenue worth a multiple: users trusting it with funds. That makes the week a natural experiment on durability.

Rules fixed in advance

I wrote the tests and decision rules in PREREGISTRATION.md before downloading any daily series. That ordering is my statement. What can be checked is the OpenTimestamps proof (PREREGISTRATION.md.ots): it anchors the file in Bitcoin block 975284, mined at 10:15 UTC on 2026-11-10. The download began 29 minutes earlier (09:46 UTC, data/raw/MANIFEST.json). So the plan was frozen within half an hour of the download: any revision after seeing the data had at most that long. The proof alone does not show the plan came first.

Pre-registered verdict: no collapse, but too imprecise to clear the 25% bar. 28-day volume came in +1.0% against the model (placebo p = 0.50). The detection threshold is -29.5%: a loss must be at least that large just to reach p = 0.10. The pre-registration required a threshold under 25% before calling the durability objection weakened, so I do not make that claim.

Position: Long, one tripwire broken. This is my judgment, not an output of the pre-registered rules (deviation 6). Long NEAR, with two exits: neutral if a second tripwire breaks, or if Intents has not set a new monthly volume record by 2027-03-31. 1 of 3 tripwires is broken now.

Predictions. PREDICTIONS.md was timestamped at 14:41 UTC on 2026-11-10 (Bitcoin block 975308), before the BigQuery data behind sections 6 and 7 was fetched (14:55 UTC), so it predates the onchain findings. It commits the thesis to three dated, falsifiable calls to 2027-03-31, scored only on data after 2026-11-10 and timestamped with OpenTimestamps in Bitcoin block 975308 (PREDICTIONS.md.ots): order flow stays above its limit, Intents sets a new monthly volume record, and the take rate stays above its calm-period average.

Evidence

1. Volume: no detectable loss (H1, primary)

The model fits log daily Intents volume on an index of comparison venues (chainflip-amm, socket-protocol, jumper-bridge-aggregator, rango) and on NEAR and ZEC spot volume, over 2026-06-01 to 2026-09-07 (R² = 0.72). Inference compares the real event window with 155 pseudo-events from calm periods.

Horizon from 2026-09-29 Abnormal volume
7 days -10.3%
14 days +3.5%
28 days (pre-registered) +1.0%
to 2026-11-09 +1.5%
  • Calm-period pseudo-events range from -29.5% (10th percentile) to +18.5% (90th). The real result sits near the median (+1.9%).
  • The first week was weak (-10.3%); over 14 days the average was +3.5%.
  • Synthetic control (second pre-registered estimate): Intents came in +39.5% against a weighted blend of the same venues. Intents ranks 4 of 5 from the bottom when each venue is treated in turn. With five units no result can reach a placebo p below 0.20, so this estimate is a sign check, not a measure of size. The two estimators disagree widely on size; the verdict uses the pre-registered primary, which is the more conservative of the two.
  • Dating the shock from the hack (sensitivity, not pre-registered): the blocking followed the Bitget hack of 2026-09-24, so 09-29, the date of the press reports, may be late. Starting the 28-day window on 09-24 gives -8.6%, inside the calm-period range.
  • Against rival venues (exploratory, raw volume, no model): the answer depends on the baseline. Against the 28 days before 2026-09-29, Intents changed -16.0% and the median of 6 comparison venues -33.1%. But rivals rallied much harder in September. Against August, before the rally, Intents was +46.8% and the rival median +89.2%. So there is no evidence that Intents beat its rivals; the model, which controls for them, says it kept pace.
  • Onchain cross-check (BigQuery, added after the pre-registration): the same model with onchain transactions relayed by intents.near as the outcome gives +8.3%. BigQuery data starts 2026-03-01, so only 44 calm windows fit, all in the summer uptrend. Against them both measures rank below every calm window: Intents had been beating its model by +7% to +26% (volume) and +12% to +44% (relayed transactions). The pre-registered test, with 155 calm windows back to 2026-03-10, puts the result mid-distribution. Both are true: no collapse, but the summer's outperformance stopped.
  • DefiLlama's volume may have changed definition. Other entries report that a large share of DefiLlama's Intents volume does not appear as public onchain swaps, with a step around 2026-09-01. DefiLlama dollars per onchain transaction rose from 1,591 in August to 2,368 in September. That fits a definition change or bigger trades during the rally; I cannot tell which. A step up before the event window would make the event-window result look better than it was.
  • In dollars, actual event-window volume was $3.71B against $3.63B predicted.

2. Fees held up, but the premium predates the shocks (H2)

Fees came in +25.2% against the fee model over the event window (placebo p = 1.00). That is not an effect of the shocks. Abnormal fees were already +28.5% in the week before 2026-09-29, and fell to -1.3% after the event window.

The take rate tells the same story. It was 13.6 bps in the calm period, 16.4 bps in the three weeks before the shocks, 16.2 bps in the event window and 14.4 bps since. So the shocks did not force Intents to cut prices to keep users. The later decline is what the take-rate tripwire watches. A fee-schedule change would produce the same pattern, and I did not find or verify one.

3. Balances on the paused networks dipped, then recovered (H3, exploit channel)

Balances are measured in token units at fixed 2026-09-28 prices, so price moves cannot pose as flows.

  • On the BNB Chain, Polygon, TON, Optimism, Avalanche, Stellar, Monad, X Layer, ADI networks halted after the exploit, balances bottomed at -22.1% on 2026-10-02. Funds were returned on 2026-10-02.
  • Four weeks later they stood at +27.5% relative to the pre-event level, compared with -14.7% on the other networks (placebo p = 0.63).
  • These networks held only $7.5M of $267.1M, so this channel could never move the total much.

4. Zcash holders did not flee, at first (H4, freeze channel)

Zcash is the asset most exposed to a freeze story: it was 18.3% of balances on 2026-09-28, and the 50-day hold involved a ZEC swap.

Window ZEC units held Calm-period 10th percentile
14 days from 2026-09-07 (forum post) +0.7% -11.7%
28 days from 2026-09-28 (freeze) -6.9% -17.4%
Latest 28 days (to 2026-11-09) -19.4% -17.4%

The pre-registered freeze-channel hypothesis is not supported: ZEC stayed within normal variation after both events. The drain came later. In the most recent 28 days ZEC units fell past the calm-period limit, so it is the tripwire that is now broken. It is too late to blame on the freeze with any confidence, and I have no test that separates it from the ZEC price cycle.

The opposite of the hypothesis did happen in the freeze window. Non-ZEC balances changed -15.0% in the 28 days from 2026-09-28, beyond their calm-period limit of +0.2%. Section 5 shows that this was mostly NEAR leaving after the September rally, not a broad exit.

5. Where balances actually moved

Outside ZEC, the largest move was NEAR. NEAR units held on Intents rose sharply during the September rally and four weeks after 2026-09-28 they had changed by -28.9%. Everything that is neither NEAR nor ZEC was +2.6%. Total balances at fixed prices were still +26.7% above the end of the calm period. This decomposition is exploratory, not pre-registered.

6. Onchain: established accounts pulled back (H5, exploratory)

Source: every transaction sent to intents.near from 2026-03-01 to 2026-11-09, from NEAR's public BigQuery dataset (sql/intents_signers_daily.sql). 82.6% of those transactions are signed by intents.near itself (relayed transactions, including 1Click), so they show no individual user and are excluded. What is left is 30,680 accounts that sign their own transactions: self-custody users, solvers and escrow contracts, a median of 130 active on a day.

The pre-registered measure is the re-sighting rate: the share of accounts active in the 28 days before 2026-09-29 that are active again in the 28 days after, against the same rate at 136 calm-period dates.

  • The drop is among established accounts. Accounts seen before September came back less than in any calm-period window; brand-new accounts, most of them from a burst of sign-ups in mid-September, came back at their usual low rate. Heavy users came back as usual.
  • Daily active accounts changed -26.3% (placebo p = 0.28), within normal variation.
  • The ten most active accounts sent 96.7% of these transactions in the event window.
  • The 2-day and 5-day tiers and the new/returning split were added after seeing the 1-day result. The query counts transactions whose receiver is intents.near, so deposits that arrive through token contracts are not in it, and the accounts include solvers and escrow contracts as well as users.

So the flow that held is mostly relayed and heavy-user flow. The accounts that pulled back are established ones that sign their own transactions.

7. The public record left (exploratory, onchain)

Two measures built from the same intents.near events in NEAR's public BigQuery dataset.

Public swap volume. Every token_diff swap event logged by intents.near lists each party's balance change. Summing the stablecoin side (ten USDC and USDT tokens on Ethereum, Tron, Solana, Base, Arbitrum and NEAR; a stablecoin-to-stablecoin swap counts both legs) gives the dollar value of public swaps without a price feed (sql/intents_stable_volume_daily.sql). Divided by DefiLlama's total, it gives the share of volume visible as public swaps.

  • The visible share first fell in September. That month every kind of activity dipped, swaps more than the rest, and it is also when other entries report a step in DefiLlama's own figures, so September alone proves little. Confidential Intents opening to every app on 2026-07-08 moved the share -4.6%, an ordinary change.
  • intents.near was upgraded on 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-07; the event format changed from version 0.3 to 0.4 from 2026-10-02. Public swaps kept being logged under the new format. The clear split starts in late October, with no further deploy: public swaps fell to a fifth of August's level while transfers and executed intents roughly doubled.
  • In the 28 days after the freeze the visible share fell 18.6%, more than in all but 3 of 256 overlapping calm windows (about 5 independent). That window contains the upgrade, so it cannot be credited to the freeze alone.
  • The pre-registered model, run on public onchain volume instead of DefiLlama's total, gives -28.6% (placebo p = 0.20).
  • What this cannot show. Where the swaps went. Confidential Intents, the upgrade and a change in how solvers settle (transfers instead of swaps) each fit. Volume with no stablecoin leg, on unlisted chains or in nep245 tokens sits in DefiLlama's total but not in the numerator, so a mix shift would also lower the share; the event counts do not depend on that. Another entry (ervhint/tideline) independently reports that most counted volume now runs through the Confidential Intents treasury.

Evidence against

  • The test is weak. A loss of 29.5% would reach the detection threshold only about half the time, and anything smaller would usually look like this result. At today's run rate a 29.5% loss is $6.6M a year of revenue.
  • The momentum stopped. Against the calm windows the onchain data covers, the event window ranks below all 44 (section 1).
  • No outperformance. Raw comparisons with rival venues swing with the baseline (section 1). Against August, before the September rally, rivals grew more than Intents.
  • The placebo distribution is coarse. The 155 pseudo-events start on consecutive days and their 28-day windows overlap, so together they carry about as much information as 6 independent windows.
  • The two pre-registered estimators disagree. OLS gives +1.0%, synthetic control +39.5%. They agree on sign, not on size.
  • The result depends on the comparison venues. Across 60 alternative specifications (donor sets, controls, event date, horizon; not pre-registered) the estimate runs from -33% to +35%, median -4.1%, and 56.7% are negative. Every specification below −15% includes the 0x aggregator. 0x's mean daily volume in the event window was 8.4× its estimation-window mean, a launch curve rather than a market-wide factor, which is why I think it is a poor comparator. That argument is mine, made after seeing the results, so weigh it accordingly.
  • September was a rally. NEAR averaged $3.53 in the three weeks before the shocks against $1.93 in the calm period. The spot-volume controls are meant to absorb this, and may not fully.
  • Established accounts pulled back. Accounts that had used Intents before September came back less than in any calm period after the shocks (section 6). Relayed 1Click swaps, 82.6% of transactions to intents.near, still show no individual users without the Intents Explorer API.
  • 28 days is not the long term. It is what the calendar allows.

At 213× revenue, durability of today's flow cannot justify the price. The price needs Intents to keep growing. What this study adds is that the two most likely interruptions to that growth have now happened once, and neither caused a collapse.

Tripwires: when this thesis is wrong

Each is recomputed on every build, over the latest 28 days, with limits fixed by the calm period. The dashboard shows the same values.

Tripwire Rule Now Limit Status
Order flow against the model 28-day abnormal volume stays above the worst tenth of calm-period windows +0.5% -29.5% holding
Take rate fees per dollar of volume stay above the calm-period average 15.0 bps 13.6 bps holding
Zcash held on Intents 28-day change in ZEC units stays above the worst tenth of calm-period windows -19.4% -17.4% broken
  • If order flow breaks, Intents is losing share to the venues it competes with, and the growth case fails.
  • If the take rate breaks, Intents is buying volume with price, and revenue per dollar of flow falls.
  • If Zcash stays broken, the privacy segment is leaving. It was 18.3% of balances on 2026-09-28, so watch whether the drain spreads to other assets.

Deviations from the pre-registration

  1. Donors. The pre-registered rule drops a donor with missing days in the estimation window. I also dropped donors with zero-volume days there, because a zero makes the log index undefined. That removed THORChain, Maya and LI.FI. 0x was dropped because its history does not cover the placebo windows, a rule I added. 0x is the donor behind every large negative estimate in the robustness grid, so this choice matters. This left chainflip-amm, socket-protocol, jumper-bridge-aggregator and rango.
  2. Placebo dates start 2026-03-10, not 2026-01-01, because CoinGecko's free daily history covers only the last 365 days.
  3. On-chain sample (H5) replaced. The keyless block archive was too slow for the planned random block sample. H5 instead uses every transaction to intents.near from NEAR's public BigQuery dataset, which is a census rather than a sample. The 2-day and 5-day user tiers are post hoc. The withdrawal-to-deposit ratio was not computed.
  4. Scroll and Plasma are not reported by DefiLlama, so H3 uses nine of the eleven paused networks.
  5. Added after seeing results: the robustness grid, the NEAR/ZEC/other balance decomposition, the long-term section, the public-visibility analysis (section 7), the rival comparison, the hack-date sensitivity, the new/returning account split, the valuation multiple, the fee-timing check in H2 and the tripwires. None of these change a pre-registered verdict.
  6. Position. PREDICTIONS.md names make fetch && make fetch-bq && make as its scorer; scoring later also needs the end date set: make fetch && END_DATE=2027-03-31 make fetch-bq && LAST_DAY=2027-03-31 make (only make fetch is strictly required, since P1 to P3 use DefiLlama). It also contains one error that cannot be edited without breaking its timestamp: P2's failure note says growth would have "stalled for 18 months"; if P2 fails in March 2027, the last record will be six months old and the first peak sixteen. Its first line also says "Written 2026-11-11", but its timestamp is 14:41 UTC on 2026-11-10: the date is wrong, and the timestamp is the date that counts. The pre-registration maps outcomes to a position only when the detection threshold is under 25%. It was not, so the position (Long, one tripwire broken), its tripwire rule and the predictions in PREDICTIONS.md are my judgment, set after seeing the results.
